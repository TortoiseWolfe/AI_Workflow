Usage gauges

At a glance: each provider has its own brass dial; the engines show how much context their turns carry.

A needle moves only for a lit dial. No limit is guessed, and the context readings are totals, never individual activity.

Real aggregate counts from one machine's usage ledger and from the providers' own rate-limit figures. A dial without a known limit shows its count as a number and stays dim.

What lit means

  1. Source: the reading comes from the provider itself (a usage report or rate-limit headers), or from a local count that has been checked against the provider's own figure.
  2. Limit: the real ceiling for that window is known, so the needle shows a percent of it. A count with no limit stays dim and shows as a number only.
  3. Fresh: the dial shows when it was read, and goes dim once the reading is older than 16 hours, two refresh windows.
  4. Proven: the dial has matched a second source it did not make, at least once, and the check is recorded in the data.

All gauge readings are stale.
